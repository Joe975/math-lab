## In plain terms

Place eight identical gravitating bodies in a plane. Some special arrangements
can rotate as a rigid shape: every body's gravitational acceleration points
toward the center with the same proportionality to its distance. These are
central configurations. Determining all possible shapes is the problem.

## What is known

Complete rigorous catalogues exist for five, six, and seven equal planar masses.
Eight-body configurations, including asymmetric ones, are already known and
individually certified. Proving that a catalogue contains every shape is much
harder than confirming that a particular shape exists.

## What a breakthrough would mean

A complete classification would settle one concrete case of a classical
celestial-mechanics problem. A new certified shape would extend the known
catalogue. Neither would solve arbitrary eight-body motion or establish orbital
stability: those are different questions.

The lab checks exact existence near numerical candidates and records which
families and regions have actually been covered. A successful numerical search
is evidence about its search design, not proof that nothing was missed.
